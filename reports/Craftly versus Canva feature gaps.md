# Local models can close Craftly's biggest gaps

> **Updated 2026-11-23.** The comparison table, its counts, the section after it, the build-order status and the list of document errors were checked against the code on that date (branch `fix/review-top-five`, commit `88c38f4`). Since the first version of 5 November, Craftly gained font upload, the Pen with point editing and Combine, attached arrows, data import for tables and charts, four on-device photo tools, a print PDF with bleed, marks and CMYK, live editing with comments, accounts with sync and teams, touch gestures and a Hindi interface. On the day of the update itself, after the table was checked, twelve more things were built and the rows they touch say so: Bulk create, a PowerPoint download, stacked charts with axis titles, fixed shapes for a crop, a link on any element, spell-check as a choice, every weight of a font, shape options, margins, a frame shape for a picture on the page, a clock and a pointer in the presenter, and a check of a design's accessibility ([`docs/TODO.md`](../docs/TODO.md) lists what is left). The Canva side was not researched again: every Canva fact is as it was read on 5 November, with the same † marks. The research sections (image models, local generation, other AI, video, the market) are kept as written on 5 November, with a short status note where something they recommend has been built. See [current features](../README.md) and the [roadmap](../docs/ROADMAP.md).

> **Update, same day, after building the first tool.** The background remover was built and tried on a real GPU. This report's default model, BiRefNet lite, does not run on a graphics card that allows only 16 storage buffers per shader (most integrated GPUs): its graph joins up to 1,024 tensors in one operation, and every workaround failed (see [`docs/ON-DEVICE-AI.md`](../docs/ON-DEVICE-AI.md), "Why ISNet and not BiRefNet"). The app now ships ISNet general-use (Apache-2.0, 88.1 MB), which runs in about five seconds per photo on that card. Everything below was written before that test; where it says "BiRefNet lite" as the default, read "ISNet general-use". The same warning applies to any other model with very wide graph nodes: try it with `e2e/ml-probe.cjs` before planning around it.

Craftly now covers the standard canvas toolset that Canva's editor offers, and three groups of gaps that this report listed on 5 November are partly closed: on-device photo tools (Remove background, Blur background, Colour pop and Upscale run on the user's graphics card), shared work (live editing, comments, accounts, sync and teams, all on a server that you run yourself) and print (bleed, marks and CMYK in the PDF). It still has no video or audio, no generative AI (no eraser, image generator or assistant), no non-Latin typography and nothing hosted: no content library of Canva's size, no publishing, and no shared work without your own server. Video and audio remain an architecture change, because the document model still has 8 element types and none is media. Of the four small image models this report proposed, two are built; the eraser and click-to-select remain, and the licence of the MI-GAN eraser weights is still unconfirmed. Image generation should start by driving the user's own local server (stable-diffusion.cpp, MIT, or ComfyUI), estimated at **4 to 5 weeks** for a first version, because browser-only generation needs a **2.6 to 6 GB** download and a recent GPU. Video is the largest item: build on WebCodecs and Mediabunny (MPL-2.0), start with offline MP4 export, then a timeline that is estimated at **12 to 14 weeks** for an MVP and **22 to 31 weeks** for a full v1 for one developer working without AI assistance. Do not try to copy Canva's cloud AI or its 141M-asset library: Canva's own AI serving costs led it to cut its growth forecast from 30% to 20% ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/12/canva-startup-growth-ai-costs-revenue-forecast-by-third/)), and on 4 November 2026 it announced job cuts affecting 22% of its staff ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/04/canva-lays-off-over-1200-people-amid-slowing-growth/)), while Canva Offline and a free Affinity already remove "offline" and "free" as unique claims, which leaves verifiable privacy, no metering, open files and a server that anyone can host as Craftly's edge. Real-time collaboration was on that do-not-copy list on 5 November; it has since been built as a self-hosted server, not as a hosted service. Several popular models are non-commercial or copyleft (RMBG, @imgly/background-removal, EdgeSAM, 4x-UltraSharp, CodeFormer, FLUX.1 Kontext, FLUX.2 klein 9B, Qwen-Image-2.1), so check every licence before you add a model. Canva facts marked † in this report are second-hand: canva.com refused direct fetching (HTTP 403) and the research agents shared one web-search allowance that ran out, so those facts come from search-tool summaries and need a check on the live site.

Nine rows are Large gaps: non-Latin text, Magic Layers, PDF import (its row is Partial since the PowerPoint export), the video editor, audio, the assistant, image generation, generative edit, and video and audio generation; the print PDF left that list. Four are Structural: template and stock-library scale, other design types, the apps platform and hosted publishing. Collaboration and sync left that list with one limit: Craftly offers them only on a server you run, where Canva offers a hosted service. Everything else is Small or Medium. Craftly is ahead of Canva in six places: version history, transparent PNG and SVG export, font upload and the background remover have no paywall, offline use needs no per-design opt-in, and the app sends nothing to a server until you start a live session, sign in to an account server or agree to download a model. Canva's paywalls are also a recurring complaint: Trustpilot shows 4.0 out of 5 from 8,201 reviews, with paywalled content, billing surprises and slow support named in the summaries (user opinion; only the first page of reviews was sampled) ([Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/canva.com)).

## Why Craftly still stops short of video and generative AI

On 5 November the inventory sorted everything that was absent into three groups ([Craftly inventory note](../research_notes/Craftly%20versus%20Canva%20feature%20gaps/craftly_inventory.md), section 8). Two of the three have moved since. The first group was left out on purpose because it needed a server: AI, collaboration, accounts, cloud sync and publishing. Craftly now has an optional server of its own (`npm run server`; `ACCOUNTS=1` adds accounts), so collaboration, comments, accounts, sync and teams exist for anyone who runs it ([COLLABORATION](../docs/COLLABORATION.md), [ACCOUNTS](../docs/ACCOUNTS.md)), and the photo tools run on the device with no server at all. What is left of this group is what needs a service that somebody pays for: cloud AI, hosted publishing and a hosted Craftly. The privacy claim changed with it. On 5 November the app made no network calls except the SVG exporter's image and font fetches ([svg.ts:114](../src/export/svg.ts)); it now also connects to a live server or an account server that the user chose, and downloads a model after the user agrees, and it still sends nothing without one of those three ([README](../README.md), "Privacy and your data"). The second group sits outside the document model and has not moved. `ElementType` has 8 members and none is video or audio, uploads accept images only, and the video export is a real-time canvas capture with no audio track ([types.ts:91](../src/core/types.ts), [assets.ts:21](../src/core/assets.ts), [video.ts](../src/export/animated/video.ts)). The third group was editor depth that nobody had built. The vector pen, boolean operations and pinch gestures are built now; non-Latin text, group editing and snap to grid are not. So the plain backlog is shorter, the server decision has been taken, and the architecture change for media is still ahead.

Image editing stopped at filters for a structural reason, and half of that reason is gone. Craftly stores a photo as a Blob and stores its filters as numbers, which canvas `ctx.filter` and blend overlays apply when the page is painted ([imageFilters.ts](../src/render/imageFilters.ts)). That design is cheap, non-destructive and easy to undo, but it cannot produce new pixels. A cut-out, an erased object or an upscaled photo each needs a model runtime that hands back a new image asset, and on 5 November Craftly had none and no Web Worker anywhere (inventory section 1, inferences). It has one now: a module worker runs ONNX Runtime Web on WebGPU, with a model store, a consent dialog and a licences screen, and four photo tools use it ([client.ts:47](../src/ml/client.ts), [ON-DEVICE-AI](../docs/ON-DEVICE-AI.md)); layout, filters and the export encoders still run on the main thread. The same design depends on `ctx.filter`, which the README calls limited in Safari, so a LUT or WebGL pipeline for new tools would also remove that browser limit; that last point is my inference, not a note finding. Canva itself does not put pixel depth in its editor. Its non-AI photo tools are four confirmed Adjust sliders plus auto-adjust, filters, a blur brush, Auto Focus, Face Retouch and shadows ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/image-editor/)†), and RAW development, curves, layers and denoising live in the separate, free Affinity app ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2026/09/15/affinity-3-3-powers-up-the-free-apps-raw-photo-processing/)). So the missing pixel layers copy a product split at Canva and are not a missing basic. What Canva closes inside the editor is the group of AI cut-out, erase, grab and upscale tools, and of those Craftly now has the cut-out and the upscaler, and still lacks erase and grab.

The two biggest absences are the ones that depend most on scale. Canva meters its AI because each generation costs real compute: Canva says its allowance limits are "operational controls and not fixed entitlements" ([AI Product Terms](https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-product-terms/)), and a price tracker reports that its free tier now allows up to 20 Standard or Premium AI uses a month, down from up to 200 Standard ([PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/canva/diffs/2026W38)); Canva's own help text gives only multipliers, not absolute numbers ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/ai-access/)). A static app with no backend cannot pay that cost, so cloud generation does not fit Craftly's design. Local models move the cost to the user's own hardware, which is why the rest of this report treats model size, GPU memory and licence as first-class constraints. Video is a different problem: the cost is engineering time, not compute, and Canva's own editor is a short-form tool. Its Help described the new timeline as beta (the latest source is from June 2026, so whether that has changed is unconfirmed), and reviewers say it lacks chroma key, strong keyframing and real colour grading, though both reviewers have a commercial stake ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/creating-and-editing-videos/)†, [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/canva-video-editor-review), [BityClips](https://bityclips.com/blog/canva-video-review-2026)). That sets a bounded target: a Craftly v1 capped near 1080p, 30 fps and a few minutes sits at Canva's own sweet spot.

## Each gap has an open route, and each route has a licence trap

This section gives one deep dive per gap you asked about. Three conventions apply. Effort figures are **estimates**, made by the note authors or by me; none comes from measured work on Craftly, and where the note author assumed infrastructure that Craftly lacks, I say so. File sizes are decimal megabytes or gigabytes read from Hugging Face's file listings, so they are exact for 2026-11-05. Licence readings are plain-text readings of cards and licence files, not legal advice.

### Image editing: classical tools plus four small models close most of the gap

> **Status, 2026-11-23.** Built since this section was written: the shared runtime (worker, model store, consent dialog, licences screen), Remove background with ISNet general-use (88.1 MB, Apache-2.0) in place of BiRefNet lite, Upscale with Real-ESRGAN general x4 v3, and two tools this section did not plan, Blur background and Colour pop, which reuse the remover's model. Of the classical tools, the 15 blend modes are built. Not built: Magic Eraser, click-to-select, curves, levels, sharpen and healing. The text below is the research of 5 November.

Craftly's photo tools are 21 filter presets, 8 sliders and duotone, stored as numbers and applied when the page is painted. Two kinds of work close the gap to Canva: classical pixel maths, and small neural models that run in the browser.

The classical tools are plain engineering. Canvas 2D already supports 15 blend modes through `globalCompositeOperation`, so blend modes need no library ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/CanvasRenderingContext2D/globalCompositeOperation)). Curves and levels are a 256-entry lookup table per channel built from a smooth monotone curve, applied in WebGL or in a worker. Sharpening is an unsharp mask. Healing can use OpenCV.js `inpaint`, which its default build includes, while liquify has no maintained browser library and would be built in-house ([OpenCV.js config](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/opencv/opencv/4.x/platforms/js/opencv_js.config.py), [glfx.js](https://github.com/evanw/glfx.js), [Photon](https://github.com/silvia-odwyer/photon)). The local image-editing note suggests Photopea's order of priority: Levels, Curves and Hue/Saturation first, then Spot Healing and Clone, then the Dodge, Burn and Sharpen brushes ([Photopea docs](https://www.photopea.com/learn/adjustments-filters)). Estimate from the note: about **2 to 4 days per tool**.

Transformers.js ships a runtime of about 4.7 MB, against 14.85 to 29.52 MB for the stock ONNX Runtime Web files, and has enabled WebGPU in Safari 26 and later since version 4.3.0 ([npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/onnxruntime-web/latest), [HF post](https://huggingface.co/blog/cross-origin-storage), [jsDelivr](https://data.jsdelivr.com/v1/packages/npm/onnxruntime-web@1.31.1?structure=flat), [releases](https://github.com/huggingface/transformers.js/releases)).

**Hardware and browser needs.** WebGPU is the main path: caniuse puts global support at 86.83%, but Firefox and Safari have it on by default only on some systems (Safari only on macOS 26 and later) ([caniuse data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Fyrd/caniuse/main/features-json/webgpu.json)). Keep a WebAssembly fallback only for the smallest models. Download each model when its tool is first opened, never at start-up, and call `navigator.storage.persist()`, because Safari deletes script-written data after 7 days without user interaction when tracking prevention is on ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria)). No source gives a controlled browser benchmark for any of these models. The only timings are community reports that the note marks unverified: about 800 ms per image for full BiRefNet on a Radeon RX 9070 XT, and about 0.5 s per frame for BiRefNet_lite at 640x640 on a laptop RTX 3050 ([runes/birefnet-lite-webgpu](https://huggingface.co/runes/birefnet-lite-webgpu), [wonfy/BiRefNet-ONNX-webgpu](https://huggingface.co/wonfy/BiRefNet-ONNX-webgpu)). For iPhones the note budgets under about 100 to 150 MB per loaded model, which is its judgement, not an Apple limit.

Do not use `@imgly/background-removal`: it is AGPL-3.0, and Craftly ships under MIT ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/imgly/background-removal-js/main/packages/web/README.md), [LICENSE](../LICENSE)); on a plain reading the note says you would have to release Craftly's source under AGPL-compatible terms or buy a licence from img.ly. Avoid EdgeSAM (NTU S-Lab licence, non-commercial) ([EdgeSAM](https://github.com/chongzhou96/EdgeSAM)), SAM 3 (custom licence and an encoder of at least 296 MB) ([SAM 3 licence](https://huggingface.co/facebook/sam3/blob/main/LICENSE)), 4x-UltraSharp (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0) ([README](https://huggingface.co/Kim2091/UltraSharp)), CodeFormer (non-commercial) ([LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sczhou/CodeFormer/master/LICENSE)), and the web-realesrgan code (GPL-2.0) ([GitHub](https://github.com/xororz/web-realesrgan/)). Do not base the upscaler on UpscalerJS; it rides on TensorFlow.js, whose latest stable release is from October 2024 ([deps.dev](https://api.deps.dev/v3/systems/npm/packages/@tensorflow%2Ftfjs)). Add a credits and licences screen that lists each model and its notice. Resolve the MI-GAN weights terms with the authors, or ship LaMa instead, before a commercial release.

**Effort (estimates).** The note gives background remover 2 to 3 days, click-to-select 4 to 6, magic eraser 4 to 6, upscaler 3 to 5 and shared infrastructure 2 to 3, which totals **15 to 23 working days**. The note assumes worker and storage infrastructure already exist. Craftly has no Web Worker, so treat the total as a floor.

**Recommended approach.** Build the shared runtime first (a module worker that owns the runtime and the model cache, a model manager, the licences screen and a device check), then the classical tools, then the models in this order: background remover, upscaler, eraser, click-to-select. The remover is the cheapest and is a paid feature at Canva; the eraser and the selector share one brush-and-mask interface, so build that interface once. Leave Magic Layers alone: the notes found no open model for it. Leave generative edit to the local-server route in the next section, because instruction-edit models such as Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (20B, Apache-2.0) and FLUX.2 klein 4B need a desktop GPU, and ONNX Runtime Web cannot load a model larger than 4 GB ([ORT docs](https://onnxruntime.ai/docs/tutorials/web/large-models.html)).

**Reaching a local server from a web page.** An HTTPS page can call a local HTTP server only with care. Chrome 142 and later asks for a `loopback-network` permission, the first call should come from a user click, and the call should use `targetAddressSpace: 'loopback'` and the address `127.0.0.1` ([Chrome guidance](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/modern-web-guidance/guides/security/local-network-access)). Firefox 151, released 2026-05-19, is rolling out a similar prompt ([Firefox 151 notes](https://www.firefox.com/en-US/firefox/151.0/releasenotes/?redirect_source=mozilla-org)). Safari is reported to block HTTPS pages from calling HTTP localhost as mixed content, and WebKit bug 171934, which asks for loopback addresses to be exempt, is still open ([WebKit](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=171934)). So Safari users need a server with TLS (ComfyUI supports `--tls-keyfile` and `--tls-certfile`), or Craftly served from localhost, or a desktop wrapper; for Mac users on Safari the in-browser tier is the only local route that needs no extra setup. Nobody tested Safari 26 or 27 directly. Each server should get a connection card with its own CORS recipe, and Craftly should keep its output sandboxed, because a script injected into Craftly's origin could drive the user's local server after the user has given permission.

**Licence warnings.** Check the licence of every model before it becomes a preset. The SD-Turbo files that browser demos load carry a non-commercial research licence, and SDXL-Turbo is non-commercial. FLUX.1 Kontext dev, FLUX.2 dev and FLUX.2 klein 9B allow commercial use of the outputs but not of the model. Qwen-Image-2.1 is under a non-commercial Qwen Research License governed by PRC law. FLUX.3 klein and SD 3.5 are free only under revenue caps (US$5M and US$1M) and carry attribution or filtering duties, and Krea 2 is gated with no commercial terms stated. The servers have their own duties: ComfyUI and the Krita plug-in are GPL-3.0, Forge Neo and Stability Matrix are AGPL-3.0, and the Draw Things server is GPL-3.0 with a README warning against bundling client and server together ([Draw Things](https://github.com/drawthingsai/draw-things-community), [Forge Neo](https://github.com/Haoming02/sd-webui-forge-classic), [Stability Matrix](https://github.com/LykosAI/StabilityMatrix)). **Safety and provenance.** Several licences make filtering or review a condition of use: FLUX Non-Commercial v2.1 requires either content filtering or a review of outputs before they are shown, and Krea 2 and FLUX.3 klein have similar duties ([licence text](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/black-forest-labs/flux2/main/model_licenses/LICENSE-FLUX-NON-COMMERICAL)). A local filter is bypassable, so a mandatory preview before insertion meets the filter-or-review condition better than a classifier alone. The EU AI Act asks providers of generative systems to mark outputs in a machine-readable way; Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026, and a secondary source says systems already on the market have until 2 December 2026, with inconsistent dates between trackers ([Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/), [Cobalt Legal](https://www.cobalt.legal/news-cases/digital-omnibus-on-ai/)). Whether inpaint and outpaint fall under the exception for assistive standard editing is a legal question the sources do not answer, so get advice. The note's pragmatic baseline is an AI-generated flag on the element, standard provenance metadata on export (IPTC and XMP), a licence badge per model, and deferring real C2PA signing, because a signing key cannot live safely in a static bundle and the c2pa-js documentation covers signing only for Node ([c2pa-js](https://github.com/contentauth/c2pa-js)). Canva draws the same line: it adds AI-content metadata only when a feature creates entirely new content or changes it significantly, and it forbids removing that metadata ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/background-remover/), [AI Product Terms](https://www.canva.com/policies/ai-product-terms/)).

**Recommended approach and effort (estimates).** Build one `ImageBackend` interface with a common job model (kind, parameters, seed, state, progress, result) stored in IndexedDB so history and variations are cheap, then add backends in this order. The closest open example is Krita AI Diffusion, which drives ComfyUI and offers generative fill, expand, references and a job queue ([Krita AI Diffusion](https://github.com/Acly/krita-ai-diffusion)). The mask and outpaint tools overlap with the eraser's brush interface, so build them once.

### Video editing: WebCodecs and Mediabunny make a short-form editor a three-to-seven-month job

> **Status, 2026-11-23.** No video work is built. One thing changed: a browser that records no WebM (Safari) now gets an MP4 from the same real-time recorder ([video.ts:37-42](../src/export/animated/video.ts)). It is still recorded as it plays, without sound, and the offline MP4 export proposed below is not built.

Canva's editor is a multi-track timeline introduced as Video 2.0 in October 2025 ([BestMediaInfo](https://bestmediainfo.com/mediainfo/mediainfo-digital/canva-launches-creative-operating-system-with-design-ai-video-20-and-all-new-affinity-10608968)). A gapless main track holds the primary clips, tracks above it hold overlays, and a design can have up to 50 audio tracks. It supports trim, split, speed from 0.25x to 2x, crop, flip, filters, volume and fades, Beat Sync (Pro), captions, and recording. Transitions work only between main-track neighbours ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/creating-and-editing-videos/)†, [Help: audio](https://www.canva.com/help/stock-music/)†). Export is MP4 or GIF, and Free allows MP4 up to 30 minutes at 1080p while paid plans allow 2 hours at 4K ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/download-as-video/)†). Canva's own help lists causes of failed downloads: more than 10 pages, videos of about 10 minutes or more, frame rates of 60 fps or higher, and embedded YouTube videos ([Help](https://www.canva.com/help/cant-download-design-or-video/)†). The target for a first Craftly version is therefore short-form at 1080p and 30 fps. Canva documents no WebM export, so MP4 is the missing parity format, and the chroma key, keyframe and colour-grading gaps that reviewers name are gaps Canva shares ([FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/canva-video-editor-review)).

**Browser support.** WebCodecs runs in Chrome and Edge 94 and later, Firefox 130 and later on desktop, and Safari 26 and later, which covers 95.92% of global usage; Firefox for Android has none ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/webcodecs)). The specification is still a W3C Working Draft ([W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/webcodecs/)). Field data from 7.7 million sessions show H.264 Baseline encoding at 99.63%, VP9 at 99.99%, AAC at 91.93% and Opus at 97.24%, but HEVC at only 77.1% and 8-bit AV1 at 87.4% ([codec analysis](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/datasets/codec-analysis-2026/), [support table](https://webcodecsfundamentals.org/datasets/codec-support-table/)). So export H.264 with AAC, fall back to Opus where the browser has no AAC encoder, and keep HEVC and AV1 out of v1. Safari needs care: the H.264 Main and High encoder could stall with no output until Safari 27.2, hardware decoding returned B-frame video out of order until 27.0, and audio encoding exists only from 26.0 ([WebKit 27.0](https://webkit.org/blog/18325/webkit-features-for-safari-27-0/), [27.2](https://webkit.org/blog/18515/webkit-features-for-safari-27-2/)). The video note's support policy is Chromium 94 and later, Safari 26 and later (27.2 recommended), Firefox 130 and later on desktop as best effort, and the current GIF and WebM path as the fallback elsewhere.

**Architecture and hardware.** Keep a timeline of tracks and clips, and treat Craftly's existing text, shape and sticker elements as overlay clips that the current renderer draws at time t. Use one `renderFrame(t)` for preview and export. Preview follows the audio clock (`AudioContext.currentTime`); export loops frame by frame and adds each frame with Mediabunny's `CanvasSource`, writing through a `StreamTarget`, and both handle backpressure. Decode with a small bounded frame queue (the note suggests 4 to 10 frames, extrapolated from one tool), close every `VideoFrame`, and use low-resolution proxies only for long-GOP or high-bitrate sources. Store media in the Origin Private File System, which 96.65% of users have, or keep file handles; the file picker API exists only in Chromium (30.9% global) ([caniuse FS Access](https://caniuse.com/native-filesystem-api), [caniuse OPFS](https://caniuse.com/mdn-api_storagemanager_getdirectory)). A 1080p RGBA frame is about 8.3 MB and a 4K RGBA frame about 33 MB (the note's arithmetic), which is why the note caps v1 at 1080p. Craftly's renderer runs on the main thread and uses `OffscreenCanvas` only for text measurement and procedural photos (inventory), so moving export into a worker depends on fonts and DOM-dependent code; a first version can export on the main thread with bounded queues. Replace MediaRecorder for video export: its output often needs repair, which Remotion lists as a use case ([Remotion](https://www.remotion.dev/docs/webcodecs)). iPhone footage is commonly HEVC (general knowledge in the note), so expect import failures on Firefox and Edge and show a clear unsupported-codec message.

**Patents.** Use the browser's own `VideoEncoder` and `AudioEncoder`, so the encoder belongs to the browser or OS vendor. Shipping x264, x265, FDK-AAC or a WebAssembly AAC encoder would make Craftly the distributor of an implementation, where the Via LA H.264 and AAC terms and OpenH264's warning to teams that build it from source apply ([Via LA](https://www.via-la.com/licensing-2/avc-h%20264/), [OpenH264 FAQ](https://www.openh264.org/faq.html)). In 2026 Via LA added tiered streaming fees for previously unlicensed implementers; whether a free tool that only exports files is in scope is unresolved ([Streaming Media](https://www.streamingmediaglobal.com/Articles/News/Featured-News/H.264-Streaming-Fees-What-Changed-Whos-Affected-and-What-It-Means-173934.aspx)). AV1 is royalty-free under the AOMedia patent licence ([AOMedia](https://aomedia.org/license/patent-license/)). This is the note's plain reading; get a legal opinion before bundling any encoder.

**Recommended approach.** Start with the one-week spike, because it answers the two biggest unknowns, which are seek speed on long-GOP clips and Safari behaviour; the note could find no measured numbers for either. Next replace the real-time WebM recorder with an offline export of the animations Craftly already has. The renderer can already paint a page at any time t and the exporters share one timeline (inventory section 3), so a loop that paints each frame and adds it with `CanvasSource` should produce MP4 that does not need the tab in front; that step is my inference from the inventory and the Mediabunny API, not a tested result. The evidence for the last row is precedent, not proof. The notes found no data that local-first alone makes people pay. Collaboration is the structural weak point of a no-backend design; the landscape note suggests excellent single-user and offline work first, with optional end-to-end-encrypted sync or peer-to-peer later ([Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/local-first/)). Craftly has since taken a third route, a small server that the user or their organisation runs; it is not end-to-end encrypted, because the server sees the designs that are shared with it ([SECURITY](../SECURITY.md)). Young local-first rivals already exist, so speed matters: Polotno App stores designs as local JSON and runs a local MCP server, Avnac and HyCanvas are AGPL-3.0 projects, and Canva Offline competes for the same message ([Polotno App](https://github.com/polotno-project/polotno-studio-app), [Avnac](https://github.com/akinloluwami/avnac), [HyCanvas](https://github.com/hyscaler/HyCanvas)).

Steps 2 and 4 to 7 add up to the **15 to 23 working days** quoted in the image-editing dive, and step 3 adds 2 to 4 days for each classical tool. Steps 2 and 4 to 9 together come to about 8 to 13 weeks of work before the timeline starts. That is my arithmetic from the figures above, counting five working days to a week and leaving out step 1, step 3 and the optional ComfyUI adapter; it runs slightly high because the mask tools in step 9 overlap with step 6.

Three things could change the order. If your users are mainly short-form social creators, move steps 8 and 10 ahead of step 9, because Canva's own video sweet spot is short clips. If your users are mainly on Macs with Safari, move step 12 up, because a local server is hard to reach from Safari. If the one-week spike shows that Mediabunny seeking or Safari behaviour is poor, re-plan step 10 before you start it.

Run five checks before you promise any speed or ship any model. Measure each model on a mid-range laptop, a phone and Safari 26, because the notes found no controlled browser benchmark. Settle the MI-GAN weights licence, or ship LaMa. Test the local-server connection on real Chrome, Firefox and Safari machines, because nobody tested it end to end. Get a legal opinion on bundled video encoders and on the EU AI Act marking duty before you add generation. Write the model licence manifest (model, licence, revenue limit, attribution text) before the first model ships, and show it in the app.

## Second-hand Canva facts and unmeasured models limit how far the numbers can be trusted

The most important limit is about Canva itself. canva.com returned HTTP 403 to every direct fetch the researchers tried (the pricing page, the Help Center and the newsroom), web.archive.org was blocked by the tool, and a reader-proxy attempt was refused by the permission system and was not retried or worked around. The research agents also shared one web-search allowance, 200 calls per turn across all agents, and it ran out part-way through each of the seven web-research notes. So the notes hold three levels of Canva evidence. The AI note read canva.com pages in the built-in browser pane on 2026-11-05, which is first-hand except for items it tags as snippet only. The core-platform note tags canva.com facts [S], meaning a search tool's summary of the page and not the page. The video-and-photo note took every canva.com citation from search summaries. That is why this report marks second-hand facts with †. Canva's Help pages carry no date, and the search tool itself called several of them old: bulk-create pages more than 500 days old, the Google Workspace and Teams pages about two years old, some solution pages 400 to 1,500 days old, the video-download, video-editor and resize pages 1,200 to 2,400 days old, and store pages more than 2,200 days old; the Help Center also still says Teams, the plan that Business replaced. Check exact limits (file sizes, durations, row counts, prices) in the live product before you quote them.

Seven more limits apply to the whole report. No independent lab benchmark of Canva's AI exists in the notes, and the hands-on evidence is one blog post. Canva's own performance and ranking claims (the DesignArena rankings for Craft, Brush being 69% faster and 46% cheaper per edit task, and its models being several times faster and cheaper than frontier alternatives) are vendor claims that no one verified. For local models, almost no controlled browser benchmarks exist; file sizes come from Hugging Face's API and are exact, but licence terms and speeds were read through a fetch-time summariser, where "not stated" means not found and not absent. Effort estimates are judgement, and no velocity data exists for Craftly. The Craftly inventory did not drive the app in a browser or re-run the tests, quotes the maintainer's logs, treats "absent" as "not found by searching the code", and reports performance and accessibility numbers that are the author's own. One note met GitHub repository descriptions with self-promotional text aimed at AI agents and ignored it. Adobe MAX 2026 runs from 10 to 12 November, so Adobe announcements after the 5 November snapshot are not covered.

The update of 2026-11-23 has limits of its own. I checked the Craftly columns by reading the README and searching the code, and did not run the app or its tests. I did not look at Canva again, so anything Canva changed after 5 November is not in this report, and the Canva columns of the comparison table are 18 days old on the day of the update.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes the question. Craftly is not a Canva clone that lacks AI; it is a complete editor, since November with shared work on a server you run, whose missing parts fall into a closable middle and two ends that are not worth chasing. The closable middle is small and partly closed: perception models of 5 to 115 MB (two of the four tools are built), a deterministic MP4 export and, behind an engine that the user owns, image generation. The two ends are Canva's frontier models and its content and hosted scale, and Canva's own 2026 shows that even Canva cannot run them cheaply. That makes Craftly's cost structure its main asset, so one test can sort the whole build order: does the feature run on the user's machine, cost you nothing per use, and carry a licence you can show inside the app?

The largest risks are facts that nobody has measured. No note holds a browser benchmark for any model, nobody tested a local server from a real HTTPS page in Safari, Canva's exact limits are second-hand, and several of the best-known models are non-commercial or copyleft, which would force a commercial agreement or a change to Craftly's MIT licence. Do the measuring, the one-week video spike and the licence manifest before the third feature, not after the tenth. The same care applies to the documents: Craftly's best claims (local by default, offline, tested) can be checked, which is what separates a trusted local-first tool from a quick AI-written clone, so keep the README as accurate as the code.
